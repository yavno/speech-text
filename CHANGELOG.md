# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Every model says whether it takes your languages.** In Settings › Speech to text and setup, each speech model shows how many languages it knows ("25 languages", "English only", "90+ languages") beside its size, and a "No Spanish" badge when it lacks one of the languages in Dictation › Languages I speak — on every row, before you download anything. Apple Dictation asks macOS which of your languages work on-device on this Mac. A weakness we measured ("Makes mistakes in Ukrainian") is shown only to someone who speaks that language, and the speech and AI Rewrite rows no longer talk about Ukrainian to everyone. While the selected model can't take one of your languages, the caption under Languages I speak says so, with a way to choose another model.
- **The recommended local model follows the languages you speak.** If you speak only English, it is Phonon-2; if one of your languages is one Parakeet does not know (Japanese, Turkish, Arabic and others), it is Whisper Large v3 Turbo; and when the disk is nearly full it is Parakeet Redux rather than Whisper Small. Everyone else is still recommended Parakeet v3.
- **Three more Parakeet models on this Mac**, in Settings › Speech to text and setup: **Parakeet Ultra**, Parakeet v3 trained further — the same 25 languages, Ukrainian among them, as accurate as v3 in our tests; **Parakeet Redux**, the same languages in less than half the download, with more mistakes in Ukrainian and a slower answer; and **Phonon-2**, for **English only**. Redux and Phonon-2 need macOS 15 and are not offered on macOS 14.
- **AI Rewrite runs on your Mac with nothing else installed.** The built-in models — Qwen3.8 27B, gpt-oss 20B, Gemma 4 12B, Qwen3.5 9B and Qwen3.5 4B, from 2.7 GB to 19 GB — are downloaded from Settings › AI Rewrite or setup and run by llama.cpp in a helper service, so a model that runs out of memory ends the service rather than the dictation. The model is loaded and its instructions read while you speak, so a rewrite takes a second or two. Ollama is no longer used: if you had picked it, SpeechText switches to the same model built in at launch (downloaded again, since Ollama's copy isn't a file SpeechText can read); a remote Ollama has no replacement.
- **Acknowledgements** in Settings › General: the open-source code SpeechText is built on — WhisperKit, FluidAudio, Sparkle and llama.cpp, and the code each carries inside it — with the full text of every licence.
- **A new Settings window**, laid out like the setup window and split into categories: General, Appearance, Dictation, Speech to text, AI Rewrite, Privacy & Data and API Keys. Each skin shows a preview of its overlay, in the colours you picked.
- **API Keys lists every key.** The ones your current choices need come first, marked *Added* or *Needed*. Each key is tagged with what it can serve, *Speech*, *AI Rewrite* or both, so a key can be entered before you pick the engine that needs it.
- **Display colours for the techno skin**: green (the original), amber, cyan, white, or any colour of your own. AI mode stays violet whatever you pick.
- **Hold to talk needs only the letter.** Once a hold recording has started you can let go of the modifier keys and keep holding just the letter, so clicks and ⌘Tab work normally while you speak. A small tip beside the overlay teaches this for your first few recordings, and setup and Settings show it too.
- **A main window**, opened from the menu's *Open SpeechText* or a click on the Dock icon. *Home* shows the shortcut to learn, your engines, the last 7 days and recent dictations. *History* replaces the History window, with days, search (⌘F), filters for AI Rewrite, edits and failures, and Retry, Copy and Delete on each row. *Usage* shows how much you dictated, the time it saved, what it cost and which engines and models did the work. *Prompts* replaces the presets window and says how often each prompt was used.
- **Dictionary**, a page of the new main window, for the names and terms you want spelled your way. Add a word, and optionally how it tends to come out wrong: those spellings are replaced in everything you dictate and in what AI Rewrite and Edit write back, and AI Rewrite and Edit are told to keep the word as written. A Replacements table on the same page writes anything your way — "кома" as "," — whole words, in any capitals. The words also go to the speech engine wherever it takes a list of them — OpenAI's GPT Transcribe and GPT Live Transcribe, ElevenLabs Scribe and Apple's recogniser — and to Groq's Whisper as a hint. An engine that takes fewer words than you have gets the ones you use most, and anything added this week first; the page says how many the engine in use gets, and one switch there stops sending them to any engine. ElevenLabs charges a little more for a transcription that carries them ($0.05 an hour on Scribe v2, $0.08 on Scribe v2 Realtime).
- **Music in Bluetooth headphones keeps playing at full quality while you dictate.** A headset's own microphone drops it to call quality — mono and muffled — and takes about a second to start, so by default SpeechText now records with your Mac's microphone instead. Settings › Dictation › Microphone shows which microphone is in use and why, and lets you pick System Default or any connected microphone, AirPods included. With the lid closed, the headphones' microphone is used, since the Mac's own is switched off.

### Changed

- **MiniMax M2.7 is no longer offered for AI Rewrite.** Groq stopped serving it to ordinary accounts on 16 October 2026. If you had picked it, SpeechText switches to GPT-OSS 120B at launch and says so once.
- **Qwen3.8 27B on Groq is estimated at its cached-input price** ($0.40 per million tokens, half its input price) where Groq reports cached tokens.
- **No more macOS "would like to control this computer" alert.** Turning on Accessibility — from setup, Home, Settings or a notice — opens System Settings with SpeechText's helper beside it, and nothing else. A dictation that could not be pasted because Accessibility is off says so, with *Turn On…*, instead of raising the alert each time.
- **Home says what is still missing.** While setup is unfinished, Home offers to continue it or skip it, with what it still lacks; afterwards it says when the microphone or Accessibility is off, with a way to turn it on.
- **Setup's Try it step, continued.** With Press to toggle, it says the keys can be let go once the dictation has started. The Welcome picture shows holding the shortcut as well as pressing it, and the Permissions step notices a permission turned off in System Settings.
- **Setup's Try it step has one way past it**, *Skip test and finish setup*, always at the foot of the right panel; a microphone problem offers Open Sound Settings under its explanation; Hold to talk comes first; and a model getting ready says the same time everywhere.
- **Setup asks for both permissions before it goes on.** Accessibility, which lets SpeechText type for you, can no longer be skipped; with Apple Dictation chosen, macOS Dictation has to be on too.
- **Setup's Accessibility helper follows System Settings.** It moves with the window, hides while System Settings is closed, minimised or behind another app, and says what to do when SpeechText is not in the list: drag it in from the helper, or add it with +.
- **Setup checks macOS Dictation when you pick Apple Dictation.** The Permissions step shows whether it is on, with a button to Keyboard settings; a Try it dictation that fails for it, or for Speech Recognition, offers a button to the right settings page too.
- **Local speech models are listed in the same order in setup and Settings**: the recommended and best-quality models, then Apple Dictation, then the other models, those that lack one of your languages last; models too heavy for this Mac wait behind a link in both.
- **Setup's Try it step, reworked.** It asks how the shortcut should record, *Press to toggle* or *Hold to talk* — at the top of the step, starting on Hold to talk for a new install — as Settings does. What to say stays on screen while you hold the keys. The AI shortcut is offered as large as the dictation shortcut was, and an AI dictation's panel shows only what you said — one line, with Show all — and what AI polish wrote. A failed dictation, including one that could not start, is explained in the step with **Try again**, rather than in a notice in the corner of the screen. **Start using SpeechText** opens the main window.
- **The Debug Log tells failures from routine.** Each entry has a level — debug, info, notice, error or fault — and the Debug Log window marks errors and faults, can show only notices and above or errors alone, and writes the level into what Copy puts on the clipboard. In Console each part of the app (Engine, Paste, Groq, ElevenLabs, …) is its own category. Debug lines, such as every partial transcript, are kept in the window only while its *Debug lines* switch is on.
- **A long History no longer slows SpeechText down.** Starting a dictation, typing on the Dictionary and Prompts pages, searching History and switching Usage periods no longer go through all of History each time: at 20,000 dictations a key typed in History's search takes about 15 ms instead of one to three seconds.
- **OpenAI is retiring GPT-4o mini Transcribe on 26 February 2027**, so it is no longer offered, and if you use it SpeechText now uses GPT Transcribe instead — OpenAI's replacement, $0.0045 a minute instead of $0.003. A model a vendor retires is no longer simply dropped: you are moved to its own replacement, not to the provider's default, History keeps its name and price, and a model you never picked yourself stays the one you have when SpeechText's default changes. The first dictation after such a move says so, once, with the date and the price difference; a model that has a newer one says that in Settings, with a switch. A model that is gone with nothing to replace it is no longer swapped for the provider's default behind your back: dictation (or AI Rewrite) asks you to choose one, and a vendor that answers "no such model" gets a notice that opens the model picker.
- **A shorter menu bar menu.** The shortcuts are drawn on the right, as ⌘Q is; the version is shown in Settings › General, where a click copies it, and in About SpeechText; and *Continue Setup…* is there only while setup is unfinished.
- **Techno is the default skin.** A skin you picked in Settings is kept; an install that never picked one switches to techno, and Minimal is one click away in Settings › Appearance. Setup's Try it step shows the overlay in the skin you will actually get.
- The Mac classes in Settings are named by memory alone ("16 GB class"), because naming a Mac model beside the real chip read as the app deciding which Mac this is.
- **SpeechText has a new bundle identifier**, `app.speech-text.SpeechText`, taken from the project's own domain. macOS asks once more for the microphone, speech recognition and Accessibility; your settings, API keys, shortcuts, presets, History and downloaded models carry over. The old grants can be cleared with `tccutil reset All com.speechtext.app`.
- **No cancel is silent.** Every cancelled dictation or edit now says so, and why. A dictation cancelled while the AI was rewriting it says it is saved in History. While a dictation is being transcribed or rewritten, the menu's recording items read *Cancel Dictation*.
- **Edit with AI works on what is selected when you finish the instruction**, not what was selected when you started. If you select other text while speaking, that is the text that gets edited.
- Clearer messages when a selection edit can no longer find the text it was asked to change.
- **A dictation made with no connection says where it went.** Instead of "Transcription failed", the notice reads "No connection — saved to History" and offers **Retry**. Once the network is back it is transcribed by itself — only what was saved since SpeechText started, rewritten by AI Rewrite if it was an AI dictation — and waits in History: nothing is pasted into whatever app is in front by then, and nothing replaces what you copied. One notice says how many dictations are waiting ("3 dictations not pasted"), and its **Show** opens History on them, each marked with the app it was for.
- **Usage and Home show what dictation cost again, as an estimate.** The figure is worked out from each vendor's list prices as of 14 October 2026 and always reads "≈", with that date and a note that it is not your bill. Each speech and AI model is priced at its own rate, including Scribe's charge for your Dictionary's words and cheaper cached tokens. A model whose vendor publishes no price is named instead of guessed. The prices are refreshed with releases.
- **Usage shows requests, overhead and limits.** *Requests* lists each model's requests in the provider's own units — audio sent and billed, tokens in and out, refusals — with the typical wait for an answer. *Overhead* shows audio paid for twice when a live stream fell back, retries, refused requests and the silence that was never sent. *Limits right now* shows what each provider's last reply said is left of its rate limits, which is what a free tier runs out of.
- **Usage counts every request that was paid for.** Each dictation now keeps a list of the requests it made: a live stream and the upload that replaced it, a retry from History, an AI rewrite. Each one records the audio sent and what the vendor said it billed — Groq's ten-second minimum per request included — so a dictation that fell back, or was retried, is counted for every request instead of once. A rewrite that a later retry replaced is still counted. Dictations made before this update are counted as before.
- **Usage counts what AI Rewrite was billed.** Each rewrite and edit now keeps the tokens the provider reported, and Usage and Home price those instead of estimating from the words. Rewrites made before this update are still estimated, and *How this is counted* says how many.
- **Live text survives a short outage.** When the connection drops mid-dictation, the live engines (ElevenLabs and OpenAI) reconnect and the overlay keeps showing your words. The text pasted at the stop still comes from the upload, which heard what was said while the connection was down.

### Fixed

- **Apple Dictation says when macOS Dictation is off.** On a Mac account where Dictation was never turned on, every recording failed at once with Apple's "Siri and Dictation are disabled". The notice now says Dictation is off and where to turn it on: System Settings › Keyboard › Dictation.
- **Setup's online lists open at the top.** On the Speech and AI steps, a list of services taller than its panel opened scrolled half-way, with the first service cut off.
- **History is no longer wiped by an entry the app can't read.** When an older version of SpeechText opened a History written by a newer one — after a rollback, or a build from an older branch — one entry it didn't understand made it start an empty History, and the next dictation replaced every earlier one. Such entries are now kept exactly as they were and written back unchanged, and a history file that can't be read at all is copied aside as `history.json.unreadable-<date>` before it is replaced.
- **A dictation is pasted even when its microphone goes away mid-recording** — unplugged, a headset that would not settle, a device that failed to restart. It used to be kept only in History, with the overlay left up. Pressing the Record Window shortcut while a paused segment was still being transcribed no longer throws that segment away, and pausing then closing the Record Window on Apple Dictation no longer writes the same text to History twice.
- **A cut-off AI rewrite is no longer pasted.** When the model ran out of room, on this Mac or online, the dictation is now pasted as said, and an edit puts the selected text back. A rewrite on this Mac no longer stops early on an unusual character or ends with a stray model token; a reply whose mode marker was written loosely no longer loses the start of its text when the text contains `>>`; and an AI Rewrite error shows the provider's message instead of a whole web page or JSON reply.
- **Apple Dictation keeps every sentence when you pause between them.** A sentence was lost when the next one began with a word that starts like its first word — "It" after "I…", "Then" after "The…", "Але" after "А…".
- **A realtime stream that drops opens one new connection, not two,** and the old one is closed. Audio arriving after you stop is no longer sent behind the final commit, and dictations saved offline are retried even when the network came back while an automatic retry was still out.
- **The shortcut recorder no longer takes a bare key.** A key pressed with only Caps Lock, fn or the numeric pad "held" was saved as a shortcut and registered a bare letter or arrow that no other app could then type; ⎋ still cancels with Caps Lock on. F1–F20 can still be used alone. Shortcuts are drawn correctly with a Japanese, Chinese or Korean input method, and F-keys are drawn by name.
- **A dictation is pasted only into the app it is for.** If that app has quit, or does not come back to the front after an AI rewrite, the text goes to the Record Window with a notice, and an edit's text to the clipboard, instead of into whatever app is in front. The Record Window's Insert no longer pastes into the Record Window itself when its app is gone.
- **A dictation started and stopped in SpeechText's own window goes to the Record Window.** It used to be pasted into the app of an earlier dictation.
- **An open Record Window keeps its text.** A dictation that can't be pasted is added at its end instead of replacing the window, and the menu's Record Window item brings the open one forward.
- **Edit selection no longer cuts text in one app and pastes the rewrite into another.** If you switch apps while speaking the instruction, the edit says the selection was in another app and changes nothing.
- When an app is slow to copy or cut for Edit selection, your clipboard is put back, and text it cut late is pasted back where it was.
- With hold to talk, a tap followed by ⎋ and then the Edit shortcut no longer cancels the edit.
- **Apple Dictation keeps every word while the menu bar menu is open.** A dictation longer than a minute lost everything said after the minute if the SpeechText menu was open when Apple's recogniser had to start its next chunk.
- **An Apple dictation is pasted even when the recogniser reports an error after your words.** It used to end in "Transcription failed", with the text in the Record Window instead of where you were typing.
- **Apple Dictation keeps listening after a recognition error in the middle of a dictation.** When the recogniser stopped with an error after it had already heard words, everything you said until its next restart, up to 50 seconds later, was lost with no notice; it now starts listening again at once and keeps what it had heard.
- A recording whose audio file can't be written no longer leaves part of it in the temporary folder.
- **A speech model download that would fill your disk is refused before it starts**, and Settings says how much space to free.
- **Settings stays responsive while a model downloads**, and installing a speech model no longer leaves two models in memory.
- **Typing or room noise is no longer sent for transcription.** A small voice detector (Silero VAD, built into the app) checks each recording before it is uploaded: with no voice in it, nothing is sent — so nothing is billed and nothing invented is pasted. A live stream is checked when you stop. The recording stays in History, so it can be retried.
- **Noise is no longer pasted as text.** A model that hears a breath, a cough or a keyboard sometimes answers with «.», «..» or words in another alphabet («うーん。», «흠..»). A result with no letter or digit is now dropped, and so is one written entirely in an alphabet none of the languages in Settings › Dictation › *Languages I speak* uses (Latin always counts). With no languages stated, nothing is dropped for its alphabet. The recording stays in History, so it can be retried.
- **The Apple engine no longer loses a dictation at the stop.** On-device recognition could end with an empty final result and erase the words shown on the overlay, so nothing was pasted. A pause mid-dictation also dropped the sentence before it. Both now keep every word heard.
- A live dictation is no longer pasted with its last words twice. A realtime engine can send the end of the recording again after the stop, and that repeat is now ignored instead of appended; waiting for it no longer holds up the paste.
- **No more invented sign-offs.** A shortcut pressed with nothing said could paste text nobody spoke: "Thank you for watching", "Дякую за перегляд", "Evet.". A short recording whose whole result is one of these known phrases now pastes nothing, and it no longer flashes on the overlay. Such a recording stays in History, where it can be retried. The live engines (ElevenLabs and OpenAI) now also ignore silent recordings and ones too short to be speech, as the upload engines already did.
- **Long dictations with ElevenLabs Scribe Realtime no longer pick up invented text.** Read without a long pause, Scribe Realtime keeps one stretch open, and past about half a minute it began writing sentences nobody said, or one phrase over and over, which were then pasted. When any stretch runs past 30 seconds, the text pasted at the stop now comes from the uploaded recording (Scribe v2), so the paste takes a few seconds longer and that audio is paid for twice. The overlay still shows the live text while you speak.
- A word cut in two by a pause in ElevenLabs Scribe Realtime is pasted whole even when the stream ended the first half with "..." and started the second with a capital: "перфоратор", not "перфо Ратор".

### Security

- **API keys are kept in the macOS keychain**, in a part of it that only SpeechText can open, instead of the preferences file any app running as you could read. Keys you entered before are moved there on first launch and deleted from the preferences, including those left behind by earlier names of the app. Old Time Machine backups still hold the preferences file, so replacing your keys with the provider once is worth doing. The keys are tied to this Mac: on a new Mac, enter them again.

## [0.9.1] - 2026-09-24

### Added

- **A disk image that shows how to install.** Opening it shows SpeechText, an arrow and your Applications folder, and says what happens next. Opened straight from the disk image, SpeechText offers to move itself to Applications and starts again from there. The disk image is left for you to eject.
- **A setup window on first run**, instead of two unexplained permission alerts. It walks through choosing a speech engine, an optional AI provider, the keys those need, the microphone and Accessibility permissions and whether to launch at login — and ends with a dictation you can try before you rely on it. Close it half-way and it opens where you left it. It is in the menu any time as *Continue Setup…*.
- **Automatic updates.** SpeechText now checks once a day whether a newer version has been released, offers it with that version's release notes, and installs it when you agree — the menu's *Check for Updates…* asks right away. Every update is signed with a key of the project's own, checked before anything is installed.
- **Local speech recognition** with open-source models, run on-device, installed, cancelled or removed with one click. No key, and nothing leaves the Mac. Two engines: **Parakeet v3** — NVIDIA's multilingual model in 25 European languages, through FluidAudio — and Whisper, through WhisperKit.
- Parakeet v3 is now the recommended local model on every Apple Silicon Mac. On a real Ukrainian dictation it matched the best cloud engine word for word where local Whisper misread a word, answered in well under a second, and downloads 483 MB rather than 645 MB. Whisper stays installable and is still what Intel Macs are pointed at.
- **Local AI processing through Ollama**: Qwen3.5 / 3.6 / 3.8, Gemma 4 and gpt-oss, pulled with one click. Any model you already have in Ollama can be picked too.
- **Model recommendations by Mac class**, from an 8 GB MacBook Neo to a 512 GB Mac Studio: each class gets a recommended model (the fastest of good quality) and a best-quality one, and heavier models stay available with a warning.
- **OpenAI as a speech engine**, alongside Groq and ElevenLabs, with your own key: an upload model and a live one that streams words as you speak. It is the newest engine here and the least used, so treat the live model as the rough edge it is. The chat models keep their own setting, and the two share one key.
- **"Languages I speak"** replaces the old single-language picker. Name one and every engine is told it, which is the accuracy win the setting exists for; name several and the engine detects the language itself rather than being handed a guess. Naming several also narrows what the Apple engine picks from your keyboard layout, so a layout you type in but don't speak can no longer decide. Your old choice is carried over once.
- **Edit the selected text with AI** — `Ctrl+Shift+E`. Select text anywhere, press it, optionally say what to change, and the rewrite replaces the selection. With no instruction it polishes; with one, it follows it. If anything fails — no instruction transcribed, the model refuses, the rewrite is identical — your original text is put back exactly as it was, formatting included.
- **Hold to talk**, off by default: hold a dictation shortcut to record and let go to stop, tap it twice to lock recording on hands-free, and a single tap cancels. While a dictation runs, the *other* mode's shortcut switches it between plain and AI, and its own stops it.
- **Escape cancels a dictation** while the overlay is up. Off by default, because for that time Escape belongs to SpeechText and reaches no other app.
- **Dashes** setting: the long `—` that speech models put where you paused is redrawn as a short `–` (the default) or a plain hyphen, or left alone. The dash stays where it was; only the character changes.
- **Hesitation cleanup**, on by default: "е-е-е", "uh", "um", stretched letters and a trailing "..." are dropped from transcripts. Words that merely look like fillers are kept.
- **A Dock icon while a window is open**, so the Settings window can be found again once something covers it. Configurable: never, while a window is open, or always.
- **Shortcut conflict warnings** in Settings: a combination already used by another SpeechText shortcut is refused, and one used by an enabled macOS shortcut is saved with a warning. A shortcut that failed to register says so under the row, instead of silently doing nothing.
- The menu bar's first line says which build is running — version, build number, commit and build date — and copies it when clicked.
- **AI dictations tell the model which app, window and text field they are for**, so the same words come out as a chat message in Slack and as a letter in Mail. Nothing is read from password managers, and only the app's name from a password field.

### Changed

- An inline dictation shows its transcript **on the overlay** as it is built — with a streaming engine or the Apple engine; settled words bright, the words still moving dimmer — and pastes it once, in its final form, when you stop. Live recognition corrects itself a second later ("…we'll do it." becoming "…we'll do it, then"), and text already pasted into another app cannot take that back.
- A dictation is pasted where your cursor is **when you stop**, in whichever app is in front then — not in the app you started in. An AI dictation is written for that app too.
- A delivered dictation now ends with a space, so a second dictation into the same field doesn't start glued to the first.
- AI dictations and selection edits on OpenAI's GPT-5.x models no longer wait for the model to think first — measured at about 0.6 s per dictation spent on reasoning nobody reads.
- The instructions sent with every AI dictation are shorter. Nothing they asked for was dropped; the wording was cut, which leaves more of a free tier's per-minute allowance for your own presets.
- Model catalogues checked against the vendors on 2026-09-16. Groq's retired Llama 3.3 70B, Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen3 32B and Kimi K2 are replaced by gpt-oss 120B / 20B (plus Qwen3.8 27B and MiniMax M2.7 as labelled previews), and a stored retired model moves to gpt-oss 120B. ElevenLabs Scribe v1 and v1 Experimental are replaced by Scribe v2, which is also what a broken realtime stream now falls back to. New defaults: Claude Sonnet 5 and GPT-5.6 Luna.

### Removed

- Message delivery. An AI dictation can no longer be sent to someone through iMessage, Mail, Telegram or WhatsApp, and the app no longer asks for Contacts access.
- Webhook delivery. The Record Window no longer has a send button, and a stored webhook URL and token are deleted at launch.

### Fixed

- AI mode on Groq stopped working on the default model for free and developer accounts when Groq retired Llama 3.3 70B in August 2026.
- ElevenLabs realtime no longer pastes a dictation twice. The stream re-states text it has already committed — grown by the words said since, and with a word or two revised, even when a revision changes the word count ("FN+Q" → "FN плюс Q") — and that was being appended instead of merged.
- A recording stopped a moment after it started no longer fails with "Audio file might be corrupted or unsupported". Anything shorter than 0.3 s is dropped quietly, and a very short upload is sent in a format the engines can read. OpenAI's live model no longer rejects a dictation stopped before its connection was ready.
- The Record Window's ✨ rewrites only the segment you just dictated, instead of rewriting everything above it in that segment's style. A rewrite that would cut the text to less than half is refused and the dictation kept as you said it — unless it went to a preset meant to shorten, such as Notes or Bullets.
- With the Apple engine, a recognition failure at the very start ends the recording at once, with a notice that says what to do — add the language to Dictation, or allow Speech Recognition — instead of leaving the overlay listening to nothing until you stop.
- "Auto (System)" no longer forces the keyboard layout's language on a cloud speech model. Whisper and Scribe detect the language themselves; being told the wrong one made them drop most of a recording and translate the rest. Apple's recogniser, which has no auto mode, still follows the layout.
- The end of a dictation is no longer lost when the connection drops mid-sentence. The upload that takes over re-words the opening slightly, which used to read as a different dictation and stop everything after it from being pasted. A dictation is now pasted whole, once, at the stop.
- Connecting AirPods or unplugging an interface in the middle of a sentence no longer loses the first half of the dictation. A headset that keeps reconnecting ends the recording with what you said rather than looping.
- A word the live stream cut in two ("…база дани" + "х, звідки") is glued back together instead of being pasted with a space in the middle.
- A muted or dead microphone no longer produces "Thank you for watching." Silence is not uploaded, and the recording is kept so you can retry it from History once the microphone works.
- Pasting works on non-QWERTY keyboard layouts. On Dvorak, `Cmd+V` was being sent as whatever key sits in V's place, so a dictation quietly went nowhere.
- Your clipboard is no longer left holding a fragment of your own dictation after a recording that pasted several times, and something you copied while dictating is not overwritten by what was on the clipboard before.
- A dictation no longer lands at the very start of a document when an app reports a nonsense cursor position.
- The model's reply no longer comes back with characters that imitate ordinary ones — a non-breaking hyphen where you dictated a plain one. Nothing on screen tells them apart, and inside a shell command or an identifier the result is a failure with no visible cause. Dashes, quotation marks and emoji are untouched.
- Closing Settings while recording a new shortcut no longer swallows the next keystroke anywhere on the Mac.
- The Debug Log keeps scrolling to the newest line in long sessions.
- Cancelling a recording while a paste is still landing no longer leaves the app waiting for it.

## [0.9.0] - 2026-09-13

First tagged as 1.0.0, renumbered on 2026-09-18: SpeechText is still in a closed beta, and a 1.0 promised more than that.

### Added

- Three recording modes: inline dictation pasted at the cursor, AI dictation rewritten by an LLM before pasting, and a Record Window for longer dictation you want to edit first — each on its own configurable global hotkey.
- Speech engines: Apple on-device (nothing leaves the Mac), Groq Whisper, and ElevenLabs Scribe — including realtime streaming with live partial text, which falls back to a batch upload of the same audio if the stream breaks.
- An LLM pass with prompt presets: the dictation itself is the prompt, and the model routes it to the preset whose "when to use" description matches. Groq, Anthropic and OpenAI are supported. Any failure or refusal falls back to the raw transcript.
- Message delivery (off by default): a dictation that names a person and a channel is resolved against your own Contacts and shown in a confirmation panel before anything is sent. iMessage, Mail, Telegram and WhatsApp.
- History of every transcription, stored locally, with retry from the archived audio for failed or empty results.
- Two skins — minimal and techno — covering the same features, with an optional accent colour for the minimal one.
- Webhook delivery: POST the transcribed text to a URL of your own, with optional Bearer token auth.
- Automatic language detection that follows the active macOS keyboard layout.
